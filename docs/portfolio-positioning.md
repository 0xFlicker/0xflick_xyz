# Portfolio positioning: Principal AI / platform engineering

Status: first-pass working strategy for human review

This document defines the narrative and information architecture for the next version of flick.ing. It is intentionally a draft: the goal is to establish a credible structure and make the important editorial decisions visible before polishing screenshots, metrics, employer names, or case-study depth.

## Target audience

Primary readers:

- Engineering leaders hiring Principal or Staff+ engineers for AI systems, platforms, infrastructure, or product architecture
- Technical recruiters who need to understand level, current focus, and career continuity in roughly 15 seconds
- Founders and senior product leaders looking for architecture leadership with current hands-on delivery experience

Secondary readers:

- Engineers evaluating technical depth and operating range
- Potential collaborators on selected AI, agent infrastructure, or developer-tooling work

## Positioning statement

> Principal engineer and systems architect building AI platforms, developer tools, and internet-scale products.

The supporting story is not a list of technologies. It is a record of repeatedly entering new computing platforms, learning the system deeply, and shipping production software across the full stack: embedded and mobile systems, streaming and consumer platforms, cloud operations, ML tooling, internet-scale media, smart contracts, and current AI-agent infrastructure.

## Editorial recommendation

The public homepage should no longer require visitors to complete the WebGL intro before reaching the professional story. The intro is distinctive and worth preserving, but it is an optional artifact, not the front door for a time-constrained hiring reader.

The site should lead with:

1. Level and scope
2. Evidence through a small number of systems
3. Current hands-on AI and agent work
4. Career continuity
5. The relationship between Flick and 0xFlicker

Crypto work remains visible as a meaningful body of distributed-systems and protocol work. It should not become either the headline or a euphemism.

## Site narrative

The core narrative is:

1. **I operate at Principal scope.** I connect architecture to product, reliability, operations, cost, and business constraints.
2. **I still build.** The House is current evidence of hands-on AI-agent, platform, infrastructure, and product work.
3. **I have operated real systems.** The career includes mature high-traffic media systems, ML operations tooling, multiplayer products, streaming, mobile, and embedded systems.
4. **The career has a throughline.** I move into emerging platforms, understand the underlying constraints, and turn them into usable production systems.
5. **0xFlicker is part of the same body of work.** It is the established publishing identity for recent experiments in AI, crypto, identity, protocols, and independent products.

## Section-by-section content goals

### Homepage hero

- State Principal level, systems architecture, AI platforms, developer tooling, and internet-scale product experience without a keyword wall.
- Establish that the work includes production ownership, not only prototypes.
- Offer direct paths to selected work, The House, experience, and contact.
- Use the first viewport as an editorial statement, not a résumé summary.

### Operating scope

- Show how the work is evaluated: architecture, reliability, operations, cost, product constraints, and monetization.
- Keep capability language outcome-oriented.
- Avoid unsupported metrics and generic leadership claims.

### Selected work

Prioritize four stories:

1. **The House** — current independent AI platform; lead with agent orchestration, multiplayer social dynamics, MCP, OAuth, production operations, and deterministic post-game artifacts.
2. **Internet-scale media platform work** — mature search/content delivery, advertising/monetization, reliability, partner APIs, and cost-aware operations; keep employer-sensitive details high level.
3. **ML operations startup** — frontend technical leadership, ownership inherited from the CTO, product/platform delivery, CI/CD and operations integration, customer-facing engineering.
4. **0xFlicker on-chain systems** — curated smart-contract, identity, digital ownership, migration, and protocol/developer-tooling work.

Each story should answer: What system was being built? What did Flick own? Which constraints mattered? What can be stated honestly today?

### About / career arc

- Connect embedded and mobile work to consumer and streaming platforms, then cloud and ML platforms, internet-scale media, and current agent systems.
- Emphasize repeated adaptation and production delivery rather than novelty for its own sake.
- Keep the voice direct and personal.

### Identity bridge

Recommended wording:

> Much of my recent experimental and open-source work is published as 0xFlicker. It is where I explore AI agents, crypto infrastructure, distributed systems, identity, and independent product development. The name is different; the engineering throughline is the same.

This should appear on both the homepage and About page, with direct GitHub and social links. Avoid dramatic reveal language.

### Experience

- Keep chronology secondary to selected work.
- For each role, prefer scope, ownership, operating responsibility, and systems over a technology inventory.
- Preserve anonymized employer labels until naming decisions are explicit.
- Do not imply The House is an employer.

### Capabilities

Use a short capability model:

- AI and agent systems
- Platform and distributed systems
- Cloud infrastructure and operations
- Product engineering and frontend architecture
- APIs, integrations, and developer tooling
- Smart contracts and decentralized systems

Technology names can provide evidence inside project or experience details, but should not drive the top-level structure.

### Contact

Invite contact for:

- Principal and Staff+ engineering roles
- Architecture leadership
- AI platform and agent infrastructure work
- Selected advisory or collaboration opportunities

Do not describe an ongoing consultancy without confirmation.

## Claims policy

Safe first-pass claims are limited to facts supplied in the project brief, existing site, or résumé. When impact is not documented, describe scope and responsibility rather than manufacturing a number.

Employer-sensitive work should remain at the class-of-system level until reviewed. Internal traffic, revenue, cost, uptime, customer, partner, roadmap, and architecture details stay out of public copy unless explicitly cleared.
